How to use Someday

A five-minute walkthrough from your first screen to understanding your results — and the assumptions behind them.

Getting started

On your first visit you'll accept a short disclaimer confirming you understand Someday is guidance, not regulated financial advice. Then you're straight into planning. Nothing you enter is uploaded — it stays in your browser.

Tip: You can leave and come back — your inputs are saved locally on your device. Use Reset & Start Over at the bottom of the planner to clear everything.

Step 1 · Who's planning, and their names

First choose whether you're planning for yourself or for you and a partner. Then add names — this is deliberately the first thing we ask, because the whole plan is personalised around them. If you'd rather not, leave them blank and we'll simply say “You” and “Partner”.

For couples, Someday models each person separately: their own ages, retirement dates, pensions, Personal Allowances and tax bands. Your names appear on every tab, chart and results row so the plan reads like your household.

Step 2 · Pensions, pots & contributions

Enter what you have and what you're adding each year:

  • Defined Contribution (DC) pot — your SIPP or workplace pension value, plus monthly or annual contributions.
  • Defined Benefit (DB) pension — a guaranteed annual income, the age it starts, and how it escalates (usually CPI).
  • ISAs — current balance and contributions. Withdrawals are tax-free.
  • Target income — the after-tax income you want each year in today's money.
Not sure of a figure? Use your latest annual pension statement for pot values and your payslip for contributions. Reasonable estimates are fine — you can adjust and re-run instantly.

Step 3 · State Pension

Add the age your State Pension starts (67 for most people retiring now) and the annual amount you expect. The full new State Pension for 2025/26 is around £11,973 a year. You can check your own forecast at gov.uk/check-state-pension.

Reading your results

Your results page answers the core question and shows the detail behind it:

  • The headline — can you sustain your target income to your planning age, and if not, when funds run low.
  • Income sources breakdown — how each year's income is made up (pension, ISA, DB, State Pension), by name, with tax status.
  • Confidence range — Monte Carlo simulation showing the spread of outcomes across many market scenarios, not just one.
  • Year-by-year table — balances, withdrawals, tax and growth for every year of retirement.
If there's a gap: try a later retirement age, higher contributions, or a slightly lower target. Re-running is instant, so experiment.

“What could I change?”

If your plan falls a little short, Someday offers a few gentle, tappable suggestions — the smallest change that would make your money last (retire a little later, save a bit more, or ease your target). Tap one to preview the effect; nothing changes until you choose “Make this my plan”. There's no pressure and no shame in it — it's just there to show you the options.

Your summary — to print or share

The “Your summary” button turns your plan into a calm, one-page snapshot in plain English — your name(s), the answer, a few key figures, and one gentle suggestion if there's a gap. Use Print or save as PDF to keep it, or to talk it over with a partner or adviser at the kitchen table. Like everything else, it's created on your device and shared only if you choose to.

How tax-free cash (PCLS) is modelled

You can normally take 25% of a DC pension tax-free (the Pension Commencement Lump Sum, up to the Lump Sum Allowance of £268,275). Someday models this marginally: rather than assuming you strip out the whole lump sum on day one, it treats 25% of each pension withdrawal as tax-free until your allowance is used up. This keeps more of your pot invested and reflects how most people actually draw down — reducing the tax you pay each year.

Remember: tax-free cash is a transfer between pots, not extra income. It doesn't appear as an income spike, and taking it doesn't shrink your total wealth — it just changes how it's taxed.

FAQ

Is my data safe?

Yes. Someday runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server, tracked, or stored anywhere but your own device.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's an educational tool to help you understand your position. It is not a personal recommendation. For regulated advice, speak to an FCA-authorised adviser. See our disclaimer & terms.

Does it work for Scotland?

Yes — select Scotland and Someday applies the Scottish income tax bands, which differ from the rest of the UK.

Can I plan as a couple with different ages?

Yes. Each partner has their own ages, retirement dates, pensions and tax allowances, and State/DB pensions start at the right time for each person.
