# One ring turns six inputs into a living answer

**Bottom line.** Someday's home should be a **Horizon Dial**: a single 320 px ring on a fixed, non-scrolling 390 × 844 screen, with the six input groups as six fixed sectors on its bezel, the monthly take-home as a serif numeral in its centre that is itself the control for what you want, a ring of **100 grains inside the track lit in proportion to the share of 2,000 simulated futures that hold**, and a 112 px linear strip of 24 faint simulated wealth strands beneath it carrying how long the money lasts and the safe figure under the worst real history. Turning the ring moves a tick scale under a fixed needle while the headline, the strands and the grain ring change in the same frame; deeper views (bands, bad histories, the edge, levers, year by year) are reached by growing an element in place or zooming out from the dial, never by scrolling; a couple's second set of figures is the back of the same dial, reached by flipping it. The evidence base is unusually convergent: avionics and watchOS settle the control grammar (fixed pointer, moving scale, visible change at the speed of the hand), four decades of radial-menu research set the ceiling (six fixed sectors sit comfortably under the eight-item limit, with the two age controls on the vertical axis where selection is fastest), the uncertainty-visualisation literature fixes the honest encodings (counts of discrete futures and soft ensembles beat gauges, crisp bands and bare percentages), and three independent studies say time series must be read linearly, not around a ring, which is why the projection lives in a strip and only the control and the single proportion are radial. Against the rejected list the dial is not a constellation (fixed positions, one object), not a form or a stepper (no rows, no sequence), not a carousel (nothing pages), not the cryptex (one ring in a stationary frame, each number said once), not a page (nothing scrolls) and not a slider stack (no slider is visible). It scores 36 of 40 against the brief's eight criteria, is buildable in Canvas 2D plus DOM without WebGL in roughly three and a half weeks, and its two real risks, radial labels and the grain ring reading as a score to chase, each have a cheap prototype test. The two runner-up paradigms, a single tuning drum and a draggable lifeline of strands, score 32 and 34; the lifeline's strand strip is absorbed into the recommendation as its data core.

## Why the market has nothing to copy, and what that frees us to do

The brief asks for an interface unlike anything on the market, and the research confirms that the market offers no model rather than a weak one. Every UK pension calculator examined reduces to the same shape: a vertical form, a single deterministic growth rate, one projected figure and a lead-generation call to action. MoneyHelper's calculator is a five-step journey ending in a table of income at set ages with a single 5% growth assumption and no ranges or probabilities, completed by **62% of the 572,000 people who started it** in the year to August 2024 ([GOV.UK Algorithmic Transparency Record](https://gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/money-and-pensions-service-pension-calculator)); unbiased.co.uk hides its single figure behind an email gate, ignores income tax and surrounds the result with "Find me an adviser" ([unbiased.co.uk](https://www.unbiased.co.uk/discover/pensions-retirement/planning-for-retirement/pension-calculator)). The nearest thing to liveness in mainstream planning was Wealthfront's Path in 2017, which put retirement age, saving and spending on touch sliders with the projection recomputing, and deliberately withheld the shaming "number you need" so that people "can explore options without feeling inadequate" ([Wealthfront](https://www.wealthfront.com/blog/introducing-path/)). Betterment compresses a whole plan into "On track · 96% chance" ([Betterment](https://www.betterment.com/help/retirement-goal)). None of them shows uncertainty and inputs on one phone screen, and none moves while the finger is down.

The same gap exists in the award circuit. Immersive 3D rose from 23% of Awwwards Site of the Day winners in 2024 to 61% in the first quarter of 2026, but the dominant idiom is scroll-driven narrative (87% of one index) and in every object site examined the manipulable thing is the brand's object, not the viewer's own numbers; the jury profile of the 2024 Site of the Year, Igloo Inc, was **Animations 9.6 out of 10 against Accessibility 6.6** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/igloo-inc); [Digital Strategy Force](https://digitalstrategyforce.com/journal/why-are-immersive-experiences-dominating-the-2026-awwwards/)). That is the genre's risk profile, and the reason this report insists the DOM and ARIA layer come first with the drawing as a presentation of it. The space Someday can occupy is therefore precise: an instrument-grade object whose geometry is the plan, on which the control and the reading are the same gesture. No product found puts six independent parameters on one dial; the sleep-schedule dial in Apple's Health and Clock apps puts two handles on one ring with the arc between them as the derived value, turning orange the moment the goal fails ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/108906)), and that two-input, one-output, honesty-cue-during-drag pattern is the closest shipped ancestor of what follows.

Two findings from the uncertainty literature change the brief's framing rather than merely supporting it. First, Hullman's survey of 90 visualisation authors found a clear contradiction between acknowledging the value of showing uncertainty and the norm of omitting it, for reasons that were partly rhetorical (authors want to signal confidence) ([Hullman 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01697)); the owner's instinct to keep bands, confidence and bad histories on the home is the unusual position, and it is the evidenced one. Second, the Bank of England's 2026 experiment with 1,600 UK participants found fan charts "well understood" and best at jointly conveying expectation and uncertainty, that the public "consistently underestimates" uncertainty, and that a point forecast which later proves wrong de-anchors trust while a fan acts as an "insurance policy" ([Bank of England SWP 1,196](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2026/anchors-aweigh-the-effect-of-communicating-forecast-uncertainty)). A retirement plan that is shown as a confident point and then meets a 2008 start is exactly that unlucky error. Honesty is therefore not a cost paid against legibility; it is what keeps the tool believed after the first bad year.

Four cautions earned a place beside the ten. Apple's visionOS guidance is blunt that rotating a virtual world "upsets people's sense of stability, even when they control the rotation", so rotation is safe only for a small object inside a stationary frame ([Apple HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.md)). Three independent studies found radial layouts slower and less accurate than linear for locating and reading values over time, with lay participants locating an hour in about 7.4 s radially against 5.2 s linearly and read errors of 41% against 14%, and participants on their own phones "universally preferred linear layouts" ([Waldner et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1907.13534); [Brehmer et al. 2018](https://mattbrehmer.ca/talks/ranges/)); the exception is a single part-to-whole proportion, where a donut is the fastest glanceable form at under 220 ms, and faster still for adults over 65 ([Blascheck et al. 2018](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2018/08/GlanceableVis-InfoVis2018.pdf); [While et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12343)). Rings that fill to 100% create "a subconscious need to complete them" and so must encode a fact rather than a target ([Gentler Streak via BGR](https://bgr.com/lifestyle/gentler-streak-gets-two-long-awaited-features-ahead-of-watchos-26)). And the Porsche Taycan's configurable three-tube cluster is the counter-example to minimalism, criticised for "excessive configurability" ([The Turn Signal](https://theturnsignal.pages.dev/driving-a-porsche-taycan-for-a-week-a-ux-review)) where Polestar's "calm" mode empties the centre entirely ([Polestar manual](https://www.polestar.com/uk/manual/polestar-2/2023/article/36c64f5c6770d5acc0a8015145cdb2ce/)). Together these fix the design rule that runs through all three candidates: radial geometry for the control and for the one proportion, linear geometry for time, a fixed frame around whatever turns, and nothing on the home that the user can configure.

## B. Three candidate paradigms, worked to the pixel

All three candidates share the constraints the evidence imposes: a fixed, non-scrolling viewport (`position: fixed; inset: 0; overflow: hidden; overscroll-behavior: none`), six input groups as fixed positions on one object, the answer at the same pixel location at all times, a count of discrete futures and a soft ensemble as the honest primary view, no chrome that carries no information, and a DOM layer of real range inputs underneath whatever is drawn. Reference geometry is 390 × 844 with a 59 px top inset and 34 px home-indicator inset, so 751 px are usable; every layout is also given at 375 × 667 (usable about 620 px once Safari's bars are allowed for). The six groups are referred to as Who, Someday, Pensions, Savings, Guaranteed and Later life; the engine fields they drive are `partnerA.birthYear` and `household`; `retireYear` and `targetNet`; `pension` and `monthlyPension`; `isa` and the cash field; `spAmount` and `db`; `horizonAge` and the `phase1/phase2` spending rules.

### Candidate 1 — The Horizon Dial ("one ring, six sectors, the answer in the middle")

**Metaphor.** A camera lens barrel seen from the front: a fixed index mark, a scale that turns under it, the exposure reading in the viewfinder. Six engravings on the bezel are the six input groups; the reading in the centre is pounds a month; the ring of grains just inside the track is the 100 futures.

**The screen (390 × 844).**

The dial is 320 px in diameter, centred at (195, 232). Sector homes are at 12, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 o'clock: Someday (stop age) at 12 and Later life (plan-to age) at 6 sit on the vertical axis because they are the two most-moved age controls and on-axis items are selected markedly faster and more accurately ([Buxton](https://www.billbuxton.com/MMExpert.html)); Pensions at 2, Savings at 4, Guaranteed at 8 and Who at 10. The wanted take-home is not a sector: it is the headline numeral itself, scrubbed vertically, in Victor's sense that the control is the number ([Explorable Explanations](http://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/)). Uncertainty lives in two places on the primary view and nowhere else: the grain ring (share of futures that hold, drawn as a donut of 100 grains, the fastest-read form for a single proportion) and the strand strip (how long it lasts, the spread, and the floor under the worst real history). Nothing is labelled "confidence" or "projection"; position carries it. At 375 × 667 the dial becomes 280 px and the strip 96 px, with the basis engraving dropping to one line; total height 568 px.

**The gestures.** A tap on a bezel label makes that sector active: the needle swings to the sector's home angle over 280 ms, the sector's small numeral and the needle take the single accent colour, and the tick scale on the ring re-labels to that value's range (Rams's binding rule, OP-1's coloured encoders). From then on a drag anywhere in the 56 px control annulus rotates the tick scale under the fixed needle; the value changes by one step per 12° detent, with a visual tick-snap and, on Android, a 5 ms vibration (iPhone browsers have no haptics; see feasibility). A full turn is therefore 30 steps. Fast turns are velocity-scaled in the iPod manner, so the gain rises from 1× below 90°/s to 8× above 540°/s and a pension pot can be swept from £0 to £1m in two flicks, while slow turns remain one step per detent. A flick projects release velocity to a target angle and lands on the nearest detent. Tapping the active label again brings the sector's second value to the needle (Pensions: pot then monthly contribution; Savings: ISAs then cash; Guaranteed: State Pension then defined-benefit pension a year; Later life: plan-to age then spending shape with three detents, steady, slow-go from 75, go-go-then-slow; Who: age then single or couple). Tapping the small numeral itself opens an in-place keypad over it for exact entry, which matters for pot sizes and defined-benefit figures where people know the number. Every pointer move writes the deterministic answer synchronously: the headline qualifier ("to 91" or "to 84"), the strands and the floor marker update in the same frame; the grain ring follows within about 100 ms as the Monte Carlo worker returns, and is drawn at reduced opacity while a run is in flight so the user is never shown a stale count as if it were current.

A couple's second set of figures is the back of the dial: a horizontal swipe across the centre disc, or a tap on the small "A | B" glyph that appears in the Who sector once the household is a couple, flips the dial about its vertical axis in 360 ms. The headline does not flip: it is an ornament floating in front of the object, in the visionOS sense, and the household figure is one figure. The back face carries the same six sectors in the same places for partner B (age, own stop year and income while still working, pots and contributions, ISAs and cash, State Pension and defined benefit, plan-to age), so muscle memory transfers. This avoids the two-concentric-rings design that the radial-reading studies warn against ([Waldner et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1907.13534)).

The five deeper views are each reached by making an existing element bigger, never by navigating. Bands: a tap on the strand strip grows it upward in place to about 420 px while the dial shrinks to a 72 px pill at the top that keeps the headline and the grain proportion, in the Dynamic Island manner of enlarging the compact presentation rather than replacing it ([Apple HIG Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities.md)); the enlarged strip shows nine graded quantile bands with soft edges and the median as a thin line, and a horizontal drag along it scrubs the year under the finger, which is the World Clock inspect gesture. Bad histories: a tap on the floor figure lights three named strands in turn, Japan 1990, the 1970s and a 2008 start, each with its own safe monthly figure; at rest no history strand is highlighted, because people over-weight any path drawn as a line ([EurekAlert, Padilla et al.](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/558221)). The edge: a pinch-out on the dial, or the "zoom" button for people who cannot pinch, zooms out until the dial is a marked point on the stop-age × spending plane; cells that hold are drawn in the accent and cells that fail in the neutral, with saturation falling where the Monte Carlo share is near 50%, following the value-suppressing palette logic ([Correll, Moritz & Heer 2018](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/value-suppressing-uncertainty-palettes)); tapping any cell steps back down to the dial set to that pair, which is Victor's ladder of abstraction descended ([Ladder of Abstraction](http://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/)). Levers: a tap on the authored sentence grows a tray from it listing the ranked actions with their measured effect on the count of futures; tapping a lever makes the dial turn itself to that value, so the user watches the instrument move rather than reading a recommendation. Year by year: a long press on the enlarged strip opens the conventional Details sheet, which the brief permits.

**Progress and first use.** A new visitor sees a complete, working instrument with every sector at a sensible default (age 54, stop at the State Pension age, £2,000 a month, typical pot, no ISAs, full new State Pension, plan to 90, steady spending), so there is an answer from the first second; Victor's relative navigation says people correct a predicted start far more readily than they fill a blank ([Magic Ink](http://worrydream.com/MagicInk/)), and Toss found that adding more to a first screen changed nothing ([Toss Feed](https://toss.im/tossfeed/article/importance-simplicity)). Untouched sectors sit "further away": their labels and numerals are at 55% opacity and their arc of the track is a hairline, the haze Muse used so that nested boards appear darker the further away they are ([Ink & Switch](https://www.inkandswitch.com/muse/)). Touching a sector brings it forward to full opacity and a 3 px track. The sky behind the dial lightens from night to dawn in six steps as sectors are confirmed, so the background encodes completeness and nothing else. The moment of completion is the sixth sector: the sky finishes its dawn over 750 ms and the headline rolls once through its digits; there is no other flourish, because rewards attached to actions are the confetti class that regulators listed as evidence against Robinhood ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/robinhood-to-pay-75m-to-settle-massachusetts-charges/248214)), and the Delight-Impact Curve reserves delight for rare moments ([Family Values](https://benji.org/family-values)).

**Motion and atmosphere.** The tick scale tracks the finger with no tween; on release an inertia curve (power 0.8, time constant 700 ms, the Motion library's defaults) carries it to the nearest detent and a spatial spring of stiffness 380 and damping ratio 0.8 settles it. Anything that encodes a value uses a critically damped effects spring (stiffness 1600, damping 1.0) so a number never overshoots the truth; the headline rolls digit by digit over 250–400 ms only on commit, never per frame. The sky is a CSS gradient between two token stops driven by one custom property; the accent's saturation is mixed from the Monte Carlo share, so the headline itself desaturates towards neutral as confidence falls. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the ring still tracks the finger (Apple explicitly allows "tracking animations directly with people's gestures"), release snaps without inertia, the needle swing and the flip become crossfades, the sky changes colour without movement, and nothing cycles ([Apple HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)); WCAG counts colour and opacity changes as not being motion, so the crossfade mode is compliant ([WCAG 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)).

**Accessibility.** Six visually hidden `<input type="range">` elements (twelve for a couple) with `step` of at least 1 and plain-English `aria-valuetext` ("Stop work at 62", "Pension pot £310,000") are the source of truth; the drawn dial is `aria-hidden` and bound to them. This gives VoiceOver and TalkBack their native adjustable behaviour, which custom `role="slider"` widgets do not receive because the assistive technology consumes the swipe gestures ([WebAIM, Lauke](https://webaim.org/discussion/mail_message?id=39122); [Angular Material #15037](https://github.com/angular/components/issues/15037)). Keyboard: the six bezel labels form a roving-tabindex tab list (arrow keys move between sectors, Enter focuses the range), then the range takes the APG map of arrows ±1 step, Page Up/Down ±10 steps, Home/End ([WAI-ARIA APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/)). The 44 px minus and plus buttons and the tap-to-type keypad satisfy the mandatory non-dragging alternative ([WCAG 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)); every target is at least 44 × 44 px, above the 24 px floor ([WCAG 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). The authored sentence is a polite live region updated once per gesture, not per frame. Contrast: labels at 4.5:1 and the 72 px headline at 3:1 or better in both themes.

**Feasibility.** Canvas 2D for the ring, ticks, grains, needle and strands, at device pixel ratio clamped to 2, with the static bezel pre-rendered to an offscreen canvas and only the rotating scale, grains and strands redrawn per frame; DOM for the headline, labels, buttons and hidden inputs. No WebGL: three.js alone is about 86 kB gzipped and brings context-loss and memory failure modes on iOS ([three.js PR 32154](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/32154)), while a canvas of this size is far inside WebKit's documented 384 MB canvas budget ([WebKit 195325](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=195325)). The flip is a `rotateY` on the DOM overlay and a `scaleX(cos θ)` redraw on the canvas, so no `preserve-3d` subtree exists and none of WebKit's flattening bugs apply ([WebKit 48386](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=48386)). The per-frame deterministic recompute is the one cost to measure: Horizon already recomputes `drawdown` three times per change synchronously in `usePlan`, so a single call is expected under 4 ms, but if a mid-range Android exceeds that, a coarse grid for the active value is precomputed and interpolated during the drag, snapping to the exact result on release. Main technical risks: iOS pointer capture leaking one event after `pointerdown` ([WebKit 220196](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=220196)), mitigated by attaching move and up handlers to a full-screen overlay during the drag; `pointercancel` if Safari decides a touch is a scroll, designed away because the home does not scroll; and Safari's 60 Hz cap on `requestAnimationFrame`, which simply sets the target ([WebKit 294338](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=294338)). Effort: about three and a half weeks for one engineer (ring and gesture 6 days, grains and strands 4, headline scrub and keypad 2, flip and partner B 2, deeper views 5, accessibility and reduced motion 3, tests 3).

**Risks and guards.** The gimmick test is Fez's: rotation must change the answer, not the view of the answer ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-polytron-s-i-fez-i-was-inspired-by-ueda-s-i-ico-i-)). Here the ring only ever changes a value, and the needle swing is the only rotation that navigates, so the test is passed by construction; the guard is to never add a second rotating ring. Radial layouts leave little room for labels and novices still search visually ([Abreu](https://lmjabreu.com/post/gesture-based-radial-menus)), so the bezel labels must stay as words, not icons, and the hallway test must confirm a stranger finds "pension pot" within ten seconds. The grain ring could read as a loading spinner or a score to chase; the guard is that it is never animated at rest, that the lit grains are the accent and the unlit ones a hairline rather than a track, and that the sentence says "holds in 86 of 100 futures" rather than any success word ([Kitces](https://www.kitces.com/blog/renaming-the-outcomes-of-a-monte-carlo-retirement-projection/)). "Cartoony" arrives through icons, gradients on the ring, or a sky that does anything other than lighten; the sky must be two flat gradient stops and the ring a line.

### Candidate 2 — The Tuning Drum ("a single cylinder, one face under the reading line")

**Metaphor.** A radio tuning drum or a watchOS Smart Stack scrubbed by the crown: one vertical cylinder whose faces are the six groups, the face under a fixed reading line is the one you are setting, the station reading above the drum is the answer.

**The screen (390 × 844).**

**Gestures.** A vertical drag or flick anywhere on the drum rotates it; faces cycle endlessly in a fixed order so the drum is one object with six positions. The face that stops under the reading line is active and grows to full size; the others foreshorten. On the active face each value is a scrubbable numeral: a horizontal drag on it crosses tick marks at one step per 24 px with detents, so setting and seeing happen in the same stroke, and the headline above updates per frame. The wanted take-home is scrubbed on the headline itself, as in Candidate 1. A tap on a numeral opens the in-place keypad. For a couple every face carries two columns, A and B, side by side, so there is no flip; the active column is the one under the thumb and takes the accent. Deeper views are reached as in Candidate 1 (grow the strip for bands, tap the floor for histories, pinch out for the edge, grow the sentence for levers, long press for year by year), with one addition that suits a drum: pressing and holding the active face flips that face over to show its own assumptions (for Guaranteed, the State Pension age and the triple lock; for Pensions, the fee and equity share), the Nest press that opens the menu on the same ring ([Cooper Hewitt](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2014/11/12/drawings-for-nest-thermostat/)).

**Progress and first use.** Untouched faces are hazed exactly as in Candidate 1; confirmed faces come forward. Because the order is fixed, the drum can open on Someday with the other faces curling away above and below, which reads as "more beyond" without a list. The sky lightens per confirmed face; completion is marked once.

**Motion and atmosphere.** The drum follows the finger directly; a flick uses the same inertia curve and lands on a face with a 380/0.8 spring; the active face scales from 0.86 to 1.0 with the same spring. Numerals move only on commit. Reduced motion replaces rotation with a crossfade between faces and removes inertia.

**Accessibility.** The faces are real DOM, so the numerals are real text and the hidden range inputs can live inside the faces themselves; the drum's rotation is `aria-hidden` presentation while a visible "previous face / next face" pair of 44 px buttons gives the non-drag path. Everything else follows Candidate 1.

**Feasibility.** CSS 3D with `rotateX` and `preserve-3d`, which this codebase already has as the rolling ring component, or a fake-3D canvas drum that draws the faces with `scaleY(cos θ)` and a gradient. CSS 3D keeps text in the DOM but carries WebKit's layer-memory risk when many faces are promoted at once, and `overflow`, `filter`, `backdrop-filter` and `mask` all flatten `preserve-3d` by design in WebKit ([WebKit 48386](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=48386); [blink-dev grouping properties](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/cubTsTuZWEY)). Keep one drum of at most seven visible faces, never a glass effect near it. Effort: about three weeks, the drum being partly built.

**Risks and guards.** This is the candidate closest to the rejected cryptex, and the owner's verdict on that ("too much hardware, everything said three times") is the risk. The guard is strict: one drum, one reading line, no second ring for views, no face that repeats the headline. It also risks reading as an iOS picker with rows, which is a list in a cylinder; the five-face stack of label-plus-two-numerals looks like a form seen edge-on. Fez's rule that rotating things should be tall rather than wide so that edge content does not move too much is satisfied, but the faces still carry text at an angle, and Apple's typography guidance is that text with depth is harder to read ([Apple HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). The A|B columns are the cleanest couple treatment of the three candidates, which is its genuine advantage.

**Gestures.** Dragging the stop handle along the thread sets the stop age (one year per 13 px, detented); dragging the end handle sets the plan-to age; both are the sleep-schedule pattern laid flat, with the strands re-fanning under the finger and the floor marker moving. The take-home is the headline scrub. Pots are set by pinching a vessel larger or smaller, or by a vertical drag on its numeral, which is the accessible path; monthly contributions are a vertical drag on the inflow arrow's numeral. State Pension and defined benefit are set by dragging the plinth's top edge up or down, so guaranteed income is literally the floor the strands cannot fall through; the State Pension age is a detent on the thread. Who is a tap on the thread's origin (age) and a second thread appears below for a partner, so a couple is two threads sharing one headline. Spending shape is the three-detent toggle. Deeper views follow the same in-place growth: the strands grow into the graded fan, the floor figure lights the three histories, pinch-out on the thread becomes the edge plane, the sentence grows into levers.

**Progress and first use.** Unset vessels are hazed outlines of default size; the thread is complete from the first second. Completion lightens the sky once.

**Motion and atmosphere.** Strands re-draw per frame from the deterministic path during a drag and crossfade to the Monte Carlo ensemble on arrival; vessels resize with a critically damped spring; handles track the finger. Reduced motion: no crossfade cycling, no spring.

**Accessibility.** Hidden ranges as before; the handles and vessel numerals are 44 px targets; the plus/minus and keypad provide the non-drag path; a pinch has a tap alternative by law ([WCAG 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)).

**Risks and guards.** Vessels sized by amount are one step from Monzo's piggy banks and could tip into "cartoony"; the guard is to draw them as hairline circles with the numeral, never as filled or illustrated objects. Six objects on a 342 px thread crowd at 375 px width, and the second thread for a couple halves the vertical room. Most seriously, the Lifeline is a chart you can pull, and a stranger may read it as a chart rather than an instrument, which costs it on the "different" criterion even though it is the most honest of the three because the uncertainty is the object itself. Its strand strip is so strong that it is carried into the recommendation.

### Why the dial, argued against the brief and the rejected list

The dial satisfies section 3 clause by clause. It lives on one screen because the longest stack of elements is 600 px of the 751 available, and depth is reached by growing, zooming and flipping. Setting an input and seeing the answer are one gesture because the tick scale, the headline qualifier, the strands and the floor are all written in the same `requestAnimationFrame` callback from the same deterministic result, and the only lag in the system, the Monte Carlo grain count, is shown as pending rather than as current. Six inputs feel like one object because they are six engravings on one bezel around one centre; the Who sector is at 10 o'clock for the same reason the FM key on Rams's T 1000 is red, so that position and colour do the work of a label ([Rams interview](https://www.designundtext.com/en/2.1.10_braun-design-interview-rams-pbdd.php)). It is minimal in the measurable sense that each figure appears exactly once (the share of futures appears as grains and in the sentence, but the grains carry no numeral, so no text is repeated), there is one typeface family and one accent, and the only chrome is the row that WCAG requires. The motion encodes meaning throughout: tick flow is rate of change, needle angle is which input, grain lighting is probability, strand spread is confidence, haze is incompleteness, sky lightness is progress, headline saturation is confidence. It is honest in the primary view because the grains, the spread and the floor are all on the home, following the dotplot evidence that frequencies improve real decisions ([Fernandes et al. 2018](https://mucollective.northwestern.edu/project/uncertainty-bus)) and the Bank of England evidence that fans protect trust ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2026/anchors-aweigh-the-effect-of-communicating-forecast-uncertainty)). And it is buildable in React with Canvas 2D and DOM at 60 fps on a three-year-old phone, because the per-frame work is a single deterministic engine call plus a few hundred canvas primitives, well inside the budget that web.dev's animation guidance and the canvas performance checklist describe ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide); [web.dev canvas](https://web.dev/articles/canvas-performance)).

Against the rejected list the argument is explicit. The **constellation** failed because figures floated and nothing could be found; the dial's six positions are fixed for ever, on compass points, which is the stability that lets radial layouts build muscle memory ([Big Medium](https://bigmedium.com/ideas/radial-menus-for-touch-ui.html)). The **long form of rows** and the **unbiased-style slider page** failed as lists and as scrolls; the dial has no rows, no visible slider and no scroll, and the one deterministic growth rate that makes those pages staid is replaced by 100 grains and 24 strands. The **carousel** failed as conventional paging; nothing on the dial pages, and the only lateral motion is the flip to a partner, which is the same object seen from behind. The **payslip and grid of months** was cartoony because it illustrated money; the dial draws nothing figurative, only lines, grains, numerals and words. The **cryptex** was the right instinct wrongly executed, and the research names each fault: it rotated the whole structure (Apple: do not let people rotate the world), it had two rings that looked alike and did different things (The Room's rule that each control must look like what it does, Monument Valley's one mechanic), and it said everything three times. The dial rotates a small scale inside a fixed frame, has one ring, and shows each number once. The **calm page with six steps** was better but still a page and still a sequence; the dial has no steps, every sector is reachable in one tap from the resting state, and the order of use is the user's. What the owner liked is kept and given a job: the ring turns, the grain ring is the rotating pie made honest (a proportion, not a target), the instrument feel comes from a fixed needle and moving scale, the night-to-dawn sky encodes completeness, and the numerals are serif.

The Tuning Drum loses on honesty and minimalism because the cylinder takes the vertical room the uncertainty needs and because a stack of faces is a form seen edge-on; it also stands nearest the cryptex. The Lifeline loses on "one object" because it is five kinds of control sharing a thread, and on "different" because it is a chart in behaviour as well as in look; but its strand strip is the most honest surface in the study and the recommendation adopts it whole. The one place the dial scores below 5 that matters is honesty, and that is deliberate: the full graded fan is one tap away rather than on the home because the cone-of-uncertainty research shows a crisp-edged band on a small surface is misread as an object that grows and shrinks ([Padilla et al. via EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/558221)); the strand ensemble is the form that is misread less, and it fits in 112 px.

**State model.** The engine `Plan` remains the single persisted object (saved by `usePlan`, migrated on load). The instrument adds view state that is never persisted and a frame-rate path that bypasses React.

type View = 'home' | 'bands' | 'histories' | 'edge' | 'levers' | 'details';

interface InstrumentState {

  view: View;                                   // which deeper view is grown; 'home' at rest

// Per-frame refs (never React state): scaleAngle, dragSamples [t, θ][], pendingRaf, lastPostedAt.

**Gesture handlers.** Pointer Events only, so the same code path runs for mouse in tests and touch on device; `touch-action: none` on the dial and the strip, set in CSS before any gesture starts because the browser ignores changes made after one begins ([MDN touch-action](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/touch-action)).

```ts
// Ring drag: fixed needle, moving scale, value committed continuously (crossing), never on release.
onPointerDown(e) {
  if (dist(e, centre) < 104 || dist(e, centre) > 160) return;       // dead zone and bezel are not the ring
  overlay.setPointerCapture(e.pointerId);                            // overlay = full-screen layer, see WebKit 220196
  drag = { θ0: atan2(e), θLast: atan2(e), v0: value(face, sub), samples: [[now, θ]] };
}
onPointerMove(e) {
  const θ = atan2(e); let dθ = unwrap(θ - drag.θLast); drag.θLast = θ;
  const ω = angularVelocity(drag.samples);                           // 3–5 sample window
  const gain = ω < 90 ? 1 : ω > 540 ? 8 : 1 + 7 * (ω - 90) / 450;    // iPod-style acceleration
  accum += dθ * gain;
  while (Math.abs(accum) >= 12) { step(face, sub, Math.sign(accum)); accum -= 12 * Math.sign(accum); tick(); }
  scaleAngle += dθ;                                                  // the scale turns under the needle
  scheduleFrame();                                                   // one rAF per frame, coalesced
}
onPointerUp(e) {
  const ω = angularVelocity(drag.samples);
  const target = scaleAngle + ω * 0.7;                               // Motion inertia: power 0.8, timeConstant 700 ms
  settleTo(nearestDetent(target), spring(380, 0.8));                 // spatial default spring
  commit();                                                          // NumberFlow roll, aria-live update, MC post, save
}
onPointerCancel(e) { onPointerUp(e); }                               // never leave a plan half-set

// Frame loop: approximate now, precise soon.
scheduleFrame() { if (raf) return; raf = requestAnimationFrame(() => {
  raf = 0;
  const P = planFrom(inputs);                                        // read hidden ranges, not React state
  const live = E.drawdown(P, { startPots: E.accumulate(P).atRetirement });   // target ≤ 4 ms
  paintScale(scaleAngle); paintHeadline(live); paintStrands(live, mc);       // direct canvas/DOM writes
  if (now - lastPostedAt > 100) { worker.postMessage({ id: ++gen, plan: P, paths: 2000, seed }); lastPostedAt = now; }
  startTransition(() => setCommitted(P));                            // low-priority React update for the tree
}); }
worker.onmessage = ({ data }) => { if (data.id !== gen) return; mc = data.mc; paintGrains(mc.successProb); };

// Headline scrub: vertical drag on the numeral sets targetNet; £50 per 6 px; same frame loop.
// Sector tap: focus(face); needle swings to home angle over 280 ms (spring 380/0.8); scale relabels.
// Second tap on the active label: sub = 1 − sub. Tap on a small numeral: open <Keypad /> in place.
// Flip: horizontal swipe ≥ 60 px across the centre disc, or the A|B button → side = other; rotateY 180° / 360 ms.
// Pinch-out on the dial (two pointers, scale ≥ 1.3) or the Zoom button → view = 'edge'. Tap on strip → 'bands'.
```

The worker protocol is the one already in `horizon/src/lib/mc.worker.ts` (`{ id, plan, paths, seed }` in, `{ id, mc }` out, stale ids dropped); the only change is posting at most every 100 ms during a drag instead of after a 250 ms debounce, and once more on release. Only the latest request matters, which is the lesson of the Plotly worker-flooding report ([Medium, webpack-worker](https://medium.com/webpack/parallelizing-javascript-workloads-building-high-performance-graphs-with-plotly-js-a9772764afe)). The safe figure under the worst history is a bisection on `targetNet` using `E.drawdown(P, { returnPath: E.STRESS_PATHS[k] })`, run in the existing `insight.worker.ts` on commit, not per frame. The edge view reuses the grid that `components/DecisionMap.tsx` already computes.

**Animation curves.** Every value-carrying property is critically damped; only the physical scale may overshoot.

## D. Three cheap tests before the full build

**Prototype 1: a single-file ring with the real engine, measured on a 2022 phone (two days).** One HTML file importing `horizon/src/engine/engine.js` as an ES module, a 320 px canvas ring with the Someday and Pensions sectors only, the headline, and the grain ring fed by `runMonteCarlo` in a worker. It exists to answer three questions the research could not: the per-frame cost of `drawdown` on a mid-range Android of the brief's vintage (pass if the median frame is under 8 ms with the engine call under 4 ms), whether a 12° detent with velocity scaling lets people land on an exact year and an exact £5,000 without the keypad (pass if four of five testers hit a stated target within two corrections), and whether the ring survives iOS Safari's pointer-capture quirk and edge gestures with the overlay approach ([WebKit 220196](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=220196)). Record frame times with `performance.now()` deltas written to a `<pre>` so no devtools are needed on the device. This is the Pad++ rule applied: "if the system becomes slow and jerky, the metaphor dies" ([Bederson & Meyer](https://mrl.cs.nyu.edu/publications/zooming/zooming.pdf)), so it is tested first.

**Prototype 2: a motion mock and an instrument-versus-flat trust comparison (three days).** A Figma or Rive mock of the full dial at 390 × 844 with the needle swing, the release settle, the grain lighting, the flip and the strip-to-bands growth, using the curves in section C, shown side by side with a flat rendering of the same numbers (a headline, a percentage and a line chart). The research found no controlled experiment on whether a 3D or instrument presentation of a financial projection changes trust; the nearest evidence says execution quality and purpose-fit drive credibility and that a glitchy animation in a money app "erodes my trust" ([Stanford guidelines](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines); [Family Values](https://benji.org/family-values)). Ten people see both in counterbalanced order and rate, on seven-point scales, how accurate the figure seems and how willing they would be to act on it; pass if the instrument is rated no lower than the flat version on either. The same session checks the two encodings the perception literature flags: does the lit grain ring read as "chance it holds" or as a score (ask "what does this ring mean?" before any explanation), and is a value read from the numeral or from the angle (ask "what is the stop age?" with the numeral masked, then unmasked).

**Prototype 3: a five-person hallway script against the MoneyHelper benchmark (one afternoon).** Participants aged 45–65 who have never seen the product, on their own phones, using prototype 1 extended with all six sectors. Tasks, in this order, each timed and scored pass/fail without prompting: (1) "Tell me what this screen is saying" within ten seconds, pass if they name a monthly amount and that it is about retirement; (2) "Make it stop work at 60", pass if done within 30 s with no more than one wrong sector touched; (3) "Your pension pot is £310,000; put that in", pass if they find either the ring or the keypad within 45 s; (4) "How sure is it?", pass if they point to the grains or the strands or quote "N of 100"; (5) "What would happen to it if markets did what they did in 2008?", pass if they find the floor figure; (6) "Show me your partner's side", pass if they flip within 20 s; (7) "What one thing would help most?", pass if they open the levers. Success measures: at least four of five complete tasks 1, 2 and 4, which is the legibility criterion; every participant reaches an answer, which must beat the 62% completion of MoneyHelper's five-step form ([GOV.UK ATR](https://gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/money-and-pensions-service-pension-calculator)); and the exit question "did anything feel like a toy?" draws no mention of the ring or sky. Any task below three of five is a design fault, not a participant fault, and goes back to section B before the build proceeds.

## What could not be verified

The brief asks for flags on anything unverified, and the notes behind this report are candid about their limits. The **Robinhood "slide-to-set" interaction** named in the brief could not be sourced; its 2015 Design Award was for restraint and typography, and the surviving gesture is swipe-to-submit ([Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/the-top-secret-robinhood-design-story)). **Nutmeg's and Moneybox's "projection dials"** could not be seen beyond App Store copy. **Nest's click-per-degree and pitch design** rests on a search excerpt because the design team's post returned 403 ([Medium, Nest](https://medium.com/building-for-the-home/designing-a-new-member-of-the-thermostat-family-36c81ebc5cb9)). The **"15% faster" pie-menu figure** comes from co-author write-ups rather than the 1988 paper text ([OSNews](https://www.osnews.com/story/30371/pie-menus-a-30-year-retrospective/)). The **Padilla cone-of-uncertainty participant numbers** are from a press release because the primary text was unreachable. **Material 3's time picker** and **Apple Maps 3D** were not examined. No published study tests **hypothetical outcome plots or dotplots on a phone while the user drags a control**, so the per-frame strand redraw is a hypothesis for prototype 2, not an evidenced claim. No **measured phone frame rates** exist for any award-winning WebGL object site; the only device-named figure found anywhere is a Cesium vertex-shader demo at 60 fps on a 2022 iPhone SE ([dev.to, GraphCast](https://dev.to/ryousuke_wayama_f134a69e4/how-we-animated-graphcasts-global-weather-predictions-in-real-time-on-an-iphone-using-cesium--3o0m)), which is why the recommendation avoids WebGL. **Instrument Serif's figure widths** are undocumented and must be checked in a browser before the headline face is final. The **Motion library's `stiffness` default** was reported inconsistently across its pages. The **iOS 26.5 haptic patch** is asserted by a library README, not by Apple. And the **cost of `drawdown` per call on a three-year-old phone** has never been measured; prototype 1 exists to measure it.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the owner's instinct rather than merely endorsing it. The drum, the pie and the dawn sky were right as feelings and wrong as encodings until each was given one job: the ring turns because turning is the gesture that reads a model at another value, the pie became a ring of 100 grains because a proportion is the one thing a radial form shows faster than anything else, and the sky lightens because incompleteness needs a cue that is not a progress bar. What the evidence forbids is just as useful: time never runs around a ring, a confidence gauge is a point estimate in costume, three bad histories must never be drawn as three lines at rest, and nothing on the home may be configurable. The resulting instrument is new in a narrow, defensible way. Its components all have ancestors, in a lens barrel, a sleep dial, a transit app's dotplot and a weather app's spread of forecasts, but no product has fused a six-input control with a live frequency display of its own outcome, and the award sites that look most like it carry no user data at all.

The decisive unknown is not technical. Canvas 2D and a hidden row of range inputs will run at 60 fps and pass an audit; the open question is whether a stranger reads a ring of grains as "the chance this holds" within ten seconds, and whether an instrument makes a pension projection feel more trustworthy or less. Both are answerable in a week for the cost of two prototypes, and both should be answered before a line of production code is written, because if the grains read as a score the honest display becomes a game, and if the instrument reads as a toy the whole premise fails for the reason the Robinhood settlement and the Stanford credibility work both predict. Build the ring; test the reading of it first.
