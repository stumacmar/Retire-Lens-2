# Finance/fintech interfaces that broke the form mould, and games/playful systems learnable in seconds — evidence for a one-screen instrument home for Someday

Scope note for the report writer. This file covers brief §4.4 (finance and fintech) and §4.5 (games and playful systems), plus the trust/credibility literature for money tools. Every claim below carries a link to a page that was actually fetched or whose search-result text was read; where I could only reach a secondary account of a screen (not the screen itself), I say so. Several primary PDFs (the Ipsos/PDP qualitative report, the Zhao & Balakrishnan UIST paper, the PLSA consultation response) could not be text-extracted in this sandbox; I cite their published summaries instead and flag the gap. Brief §2 context: an unbiased.co.uk-style slider page was judged "a bag of spanners", so the finance section concentrates on what each product did that is *not* a long form, and on which parts users trusted.

## Q1. Robinhood's early interactions: what was not a form, what was liked, and what was later judged untrustworthy

### Takeaway
Robinhood's 2015 Apple Design Award was for restraint ("clean, content-centric design, beautiful typography"), not for gimmicks; the celebratory layer added later (confetti, scratch cards, "most popular" lists) became the regulator's exhibit list and was removed. The durable lesson for Someday is that gesture-as-commitment (swipe to submit) and whole-screen colour-as-state survived; decoration that rewarded action did not. I could not verify any "radial goal picker" in early Robinhood — treat that memory as unsupported.

### Cited Findings
- Robinhood's own design history: the iOS app won the 2015 Apple Design Award for "clean, content-centric design, and beautiful typography that nicely balances app branding and iOS design conventions"; the company says the goal was design "that's friendly, that's inviting, that doesn't intimidate you, that isn't condescending"; it also states it removed confetti in 2021 and that notifications were only added in 2016 — [Robinhood newsroom, "The top-secret Robinhood design story" (14 Apr 2021)](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/the-top-secret-robinhood-design-story)
- Early design method: the founding team showed mock-ups to Stanford students in coffee shops; a 2019 Chinese-language teardown describes the app as using animation to guide users to core features and swipe gestures for navigation — [search summary of Bloomberg/woshipm coverage](https://www.woshipm.com/pd/1492995.html)
- Confetti celebrated "first trades, first steps with cash management, and successful referrals"; Robinhood replaced it with "new, dynamic visual experiences that cheer on customers" and framed removal as eliminating a "distraction"; Massachusetts regulators' complaint alleged "use of gamification strategies to manipulate customers" — [CNBC, 31 Mar 2021](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/31/robinhood-gets-rid-of-confetti-feature-amid-scrutiny-over-gamification.html)
- The Massachusetts Securities Division's evidence list "included confetti animation, digital scratch tickets, free stock rewards, and other game-like features", plus push notifications and "most popular" lists; the January 2024 settlement was $7.5m and included stopping celebratory imagery for Massachusetts accounts; Robinhood: "We reject the premise that any part of our app, past or present, is 'gamified.'" — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/robinhood-to-pay-75m-to-settle-massachusetts-charges/248214); [NBC Boston](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/business/money-report/robinhood-gets-rid-of-confetti-feature-amid-scrutiny-over-gamification-of-investing/2343214/)
- The swipe-up-to-submit order gesture still exists in Robinhood's futures flow ("review, then swipe up to place the order"); a 2021 AppleVis thread reports the swipe prompt was hard for VoiceOver users (workaround: double-tap-and-hold then swipe up) — [Robinhood futures orders help](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/futures-orders); [AppleVis thread](https://www.applevis.com/comment/122132)
- A critic's reading: "a swipe up serves to confirm a trade, and the simplicity of that gesture is what prompts users to do so" — [Medium, "Robinhood's Gamification of Investing"](https://medium.com/si-410-ethics-and-information-technology/robinhoods-gamification-of-investing-517d9db70c4c)
- Pratt design critique (2021): praises red/green as a "natural mapping" needing little thought, but wants a second signifier (arrows) so movement is readable without colour; criticises unlabelled tab icons — [Pratt IxD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2021/02/design-critique-robinhood-app-ios-app/)

### Inferences
- The parts of Robinhood that broke the form mould and survived scrutiny are *structural*: one number dominating the screen, whole-interface colour as state, a chart you scrub, and a single committing gesture. The parts that failed were *rewards attached to actions*. For Someday, a dial that moves the answer is structural; a flourish when the plan "completes" is the confetti class and should be avoided or made informational (e.g. the sky brightening because confidence rose, not because the user tapped).
- Swipe-to-commit has a known screen-reader cost; any custom gesture in Someday needs an equivalent discrete control (brief §3 accessibility).

### Gaps
- No primary source for an early Robinhood "slide to set" or radial goal picker was found; the Bloomberg 2021 design feature was not fetchable. Treat the brief's phrasing as unverified.

## Q2. Robo-adviser and planner projections: Wealthfront Path, Betterment, Empower/Personal Capital, Boldin/NewRetirement, ProjectionLab

### Takeaway
The only mainstream planner that put *input and projection in one gesture* was Wealthfront Path (2017): touch sliders for retirement age, savings and spending with the projection recomputed, and a deliberate refusal to lead with "the number you need". Betterment reduces the whole plan to one probability word-pair ("On track / 96% chance"); ProjectionLab and Boldin remain long forms with a Monte Carlo page at the end. None show uncertainty *and* inputs on one phone screen.

### Cited Findings
- Wealthfront Path (launch post, 2 Feb 2017): "the first fully mobile financial planning experience"; adjustable inputs are monthly savings, retirement spending and retirement age; assumptions (Social Security, inflation, returns) are handled by Path; users hover the graph to see values and must click a dotted line to reveal the monthly saving needed for 100% — withholding that number is "intentional" so users "can explore options without feeling inadequate" — [Wealthfront blog, "Introducing Path"](https://www.wealthfront.com/blog/introducing-path/)
- Press account: Path was "designed specifically for use on a mobile touchscreen, with factors like retirement age, savings and spending all adjustable by sliding scales"; "We don't want to be tone deaf if you log in and see you're supposed be saving $4,000, but you can't afford it" (Dan Carroll) — [WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/technology/wealthfronts-big-bet-automated-financial-planning); [InvestmentNews 2018 on home-purchase sliders](https://www.investmentnews.com/fintech/wealthfront-app-to-help-millennials-with-this-lofty-goal/73280)
- Independent verdict: Path is "not bad" but "nowhere close to the free, award-winning, financial tool that Personal Capital introduced back in 2011" — [Financial Samurai review](https://www.financialsamurai.com/wealthfront-path-review-financial-planning-tool)
- Betterment: goals are labelled "On-Track" or "Off-Track"; a 401(k) screenshot shows "On track" next to "96% chance"; recommended contributions use a 50% likelihood; projections are shown in nominal terms; a demo shows moving retirement age 65→67 flipping off-track to on-track — [Betterment help: retirement goal](https://www.betterment.com/help/retirement-goal); [Betterment Investing Tools PDF](https://www.betterment.com/hubfs/PDFs/b4b/employee-engagement/Investing%20Tools.pdf); [Goal projection disclosure](https://www.betterment.com/legal/goal-projection)
- Betterment's only true slider is stock/bond allocation, moved "until they find the allocation that has the expected range of growth outcomes they are [comfortable with]" — [Betterment goals and advice](https://www.betterment.com/category/goals-and-advice)
- Empower (ex-Personal Capital): Monte Carlo over linked accounts; reviewers call it "among the best charts, graphs, and user interface of any retirement planner"; multiple scenarios compared side by side; onboarding "could be smoother" — [Financial Samurai review](https://www.financialsamurai.com/personal-capital-retirement-planner-review); [Benzinga test](https://www.benzinga.com/money/empower-personal-dashboard-review-retirement-planner-tool-tested)
- Boldin runs 1,000 Monte Carlo paths on a paid tier; ProjectionLab up to 2,000 trials plus historical back-testing on the free tier; its 2022 redesign aimed to "work better on mobile, reduce clutter, and bring the key metrics into focus" and lets users "choose which metrics to plot and which statistics to compute" — [Boldin vs ProjectionLab](https://www.boldin.com/retirement/boldin-vs-projectionlab/); [ProjectionLab UI refresh](https://projectionlab.com/blog/ui-redesign); [Rob Berger Boldin review](https://robberger.com/?p=5431)

### Inferences
- Path's two decisions are directly transferable: (1) the three inputs the user most wants to move (stop age, spend, saving) are on sliders with the projection live; (2) the shaming number is not the headline. Someday's headline is "£/month after tax, for life", which is the user's own figure, not a verdict — this sidesteps Path's tone problem while keeping its liveness.
- Betterment shows one honest compression works: a single probability with a two-state label. Someday's "confidence across 2,000 futures" belongs at the same visual weight as the £ figure.
- ProjectionLab's "1,000–2,000 simulated paths" is the norm; Someday's 2,000 is credible but not a differentiator. The differentiator is showing it *on the home screen while the hand is on the control*, which none of these do.

### Gaps
- No screenshots/video of Path's slider screen were directly examined (reviews only). No independent usability data on any of these planners' Monte Carlo displays.

## Q3. UK retail fintech: Monzo/Revolut pots, PensionBee, Moneybox, Nutmeg, Penfold, Moneyfarm, Vanguard UK, Hargreaves Lansdown, MoneyHelper, unbiased.co.uk

### Takeaway
UK consumer finance has produced one durable non-form idea — Monzo's Pots as named containers with a goal and progress bar — and otherwise a wall of near-identical slider calculators that reduce to a single projected figure, often behind an email gate (unbiased) and with one deterministic growth assumption (MoneyHelper 5%, unbiased 5%). The staid verdict in brief §2 is consistent with the evidence: these tools never show a range, never move while the hand is on the control, and lead into adviser lead-generation.

### Cited Findings
- Monzo Pots launched 20 Nov 2017 on iOS as "separate spaces inside a Monzo account for setting money aside", "digital piggy banks" — [Monzo, "Budget like a pro with Pots"](https://monzo.com/blog/2017/11/20/pots); [Android follow-up](https://monzo.com/blog/2017/12/07/pots-android/)
- Savings goals on Pots (Jul 2018): "We'll show you the goal for each pot underneath the amount you've saved so far", with "a progress bar", and a notification "so you can celebrate!"; the origin given is user requests, not formal research — [Monzo, "Set savings goals"](https://monzo.com/blog/2018/07/19/set-savings-goals)
- Community reaction to a fan-made "pot target" concept flagged "sideways scrolling as a pain point" and asked for an overview screen — [Monzo community thread](https://community.monzo.com/t/pot-target-design-idea/30795)
- Revolut vs Monzo (forums, partisan): Revolut "so cluttered and full of unnecessary features that even basic functions are hard to locate"; one user "noticed some dark patterns nudging toward the paid Premium tier"; a counter-view finds Revolut "cleaner, easier to navigate, and more mature" — [Revolut community](https://community.revolut.com/t/stop-updating-ui-with-a-terrible-ux-changes/125058?page=7); [Monzo community](https://community.monzo.com/t/revolut-user-very-disappointed-with-monzo/7427)
- MoneyHelper pension calculator (MaPS, built in-house): five-step journey (About You → Your Income → Contributions → Lump Sum & Other Income → Results); results are a *table of income at set ages by source* with shortfall/surplus against target; "sliders and input fields let users change retirement age, contributions, employer contributions, and the lump sum percentage"; single projection, no ranges or probabilities; assumptions 5% growth, 2.5% inflation, 0.75% charges; 572k starts / 357k completions in Sept 2023–Aug 2024 (62%) — [GOV.UK Algorithmic Transparency Record](https://gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/money-and-pensions-service-pension-calculator); [GAD quality assurance](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/quality-assurance-of-pension-calculator-tool)
- Rest Less on MoneyHelper: "thorough, data-driven" but (at time of review) "no option to allow you to factor in the State Pension" — note the ATR says State Pension *is* integrated, so the review may be stale; the same roundup praises Aviva's chart showing "the difference that fund performance can have on your pension's longevity" — [Rest Less, best pension calculators](https://restless.co.uk/pensions-retirement-planning/approaching-retirement/best-pension-calculators)
- unbiased.co.uk calculator: inputs are age, retirement age, salary, pot, tax-free cash %, personal/employer contribution %, State Pension toggle; results hidden until an email is given ("Get results and an action plan"); assumptions 5% growth, 2% inflation; "does not account for income tax"; repeated "Find me an adviser" CTAs; cites "more than 10 million people" and a Trustpilot score — [unbiased.co.uk pension calculator](https://www.unbiased.co.uk/discover/pensions-retirement/planning-for-retirement/pension-calculator)
- PensionBee: UK App Store rating 3.3/5 from 51 ratings with crash complaints; Trustpilot 4.6/5 (13k+); its calculator is marketed as a way "to see how long your pension savings could last"; no calculator-specific complaints surfaced — [PensionBee App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/pensionbee/id1345490587); [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/pensionbee.com?page=7)
- Moneybox pension: in-app you can "view pension performance, tweak contribution levels, change pension fund"; no drawdown; no projection dial documented — [Finder Moneybox pension review](https://www.finder.com/uk/pensions/moneybox-pension-review)
- Nutmeg: App Store copy promises "interactive projection graphs" to "see if you're on track"; a user review calls the projection "helpful to make sure I'm on track"; no design teardown exists — [Nutmeg App Store listing via AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/nutmeg-saving-investment/1127250193?country=gb)
- Hargreaves Lansdown: an "Income Calculator to plan withdrawals"; fees "among the highest"; no calculator teardown found — [Compare Drawdown HL](https://comparedrawdown.co.uk/provider/hargreaves-lansdown)

### Inferences
- The UK calculator genre converges on: a vertical form, a single deterministic growth rate, a single projected annual figure, and an adviser CTA. The brief's rejection of the unbiased-style page is well founded: that genre's *only* gesture is a slider that redraws a number below it.
- Monzo's Pot is the only UK pattern where money is *a thing with a shape* (container, fill level, name). The transferable idea is the container-with-fill, not the list of containers. Someday's six input groups could each be a "vessel" on one instrument — but note the community complaint about sideways scrolling between pots: six vessels must be visible at once.
- MoneyHelper's 62% completion over a five-step form is a useful benchmark: a one-screen instrument should beat 62% "reached an answer" in hallway tests, or it has failed on legibility.

### Gaps
- Penfold, Moneyfarm, Vanguard UK calculators: no teardown or screens reachable; not assessed. Nutmeg's projection fan (brief §4.4 "projection dials") could not be verified beyond App Store copy.

## Q4. Japanese and Korean bank/pension apps (Toss, KakaoBank, MUFG, SBI)

### Takeaway
Toss's documented principle is "a state where nothing needs to be learned to use the service" — one input on the first screen (the amount), authentication deferred as late as possible, one action per screen. KakaoBank's distinctiveness is tonal (characters, friendliness), not interactional, and by 2022 commentators said it was hard to tell from incumbents. Neither offers a retirement instrument; the transferable lesson is Toss's "one field first, everything else later".

### Cited Findings
- Toss defines simplicity as "본능적으로 이해할 수 있는 상태" (a state one can understand instinctively); the transfer flow's first screen has one input (the amount); each sign-up screen allows one action; identity verification is asked "as late as possible"; an experiment adding more services to the first screen "had no effect on users or revenue", after which Toss removed recipient selection from the first screen — [Toss Feed, 토스는 왜 단순함에 집착할까?](https://toss.im/tossfeed/article/importance-simplicity)
- Toss began as P2P transfer; "one could make small transfers with just a fingerprint at launch"; it grew to 23m+ MAU; a 2026 expat guide ranks Toss Bank above KakaoBank for interface quality — [DigitalInAsia](https://digitalinasia.com/?p=11307); [Baillie Gifford 2026 note](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/uk/intermediaries/insights/ic-article/2026-q1-toss-inside-korea-s-financial-super-app-10060129/); [Korea Experience guide](https://korea-experience.pages.dev/blog/korean-banking-apps-best-user-interface-for-foreigners-2026)
- KakaoBank: design team "focus[es] on creating a friendly and approachable experience" against "the rigid and formal perception" of finance; a designer: "Our aim is for the app to have consistency — to make it seem like one person did the design for everything"; Kakao Friends characters drove growth (a "Chief Ryan Officer" title was created); a 2022 critique: "after five years, it is difficult to feel a difference from regulated banks" — [Figma customer story](https://www.figma.com/customers/kakaobank-faster-design-delivery-with-figma/); [Korea Times interview](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/banking-finance/20191202/interview-kakao-banks-innovation-comes-from-observing-detecting-trends); [Asia Business Daily 2022](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2022022308470095306)
- Japan: SMBC admitted its app "design was rather old, and the UX had not significantly changed" in a decade; MUFG plans a digital bank with Google in FY2026; Japan Post Bank's bankbook app was designed by 4digit; Goodpatch worked on SBI Securities UX in 2017 — [SMBC DX interview](https://www.smfg.co.jp/english/dx_link/article/0056.html); [4digit JP Bank app](https://www.4digit.com/en/projects/jp-bank-app/); [The Bridge on Goodpatch](https://thebridge.jp/en/2017/04/goodpatch-series-c-round-funding)

### Inferences
- Toss's measured finding (more on the first screen changed nothing) supports the brief's "nothing said twice" criterion with data, not taste.
- The Korean/Japanese "non-Western UI" the brief hoped for is mostly tone and character, which the brief's owner would call "cartoony". Do not import it.

### Gaps
- No Korean or Japanese *pension projection* interface was found in English-language sources; MUFG/SBI app screens were not examined.

## Q5. The UK Pensions Dashboards Programme and MoneyHelper dashboard: what research says users want to see and trust

### Takeaway
The PDP's 110-interview Ipsos study found users want accrued and projected values, start date and retirement age, and that trust rides on government positioning and data-security reassurance, not on visual novelty. Industry bodies say the hard display problem is Estimated Retirement Income (ERI): inconsistent across DB/DC, and a zero can mislead. For Someday this is a licence to make the projected monthly income the hero — and a warning to label its basis.

### Cited Findings
- Ipsos for MaPS/PDP (published 31 Jan 2022): 110 in-depth interviews (50 in Feb–Mar 2021, 60 in Aug–Sep 2021); users wanted "accrued and projected pension values, along with the pension start date and retirement age"; the concept "resonates most with people who have multiple pots and those in mid-to-late career"; government positioning "added credibility and eased concerns about data, confidentiality, security"; brand familiarity and endorsement from trusted sources helped legitimacy — [Ipsos summary page](https://www.ipsos.com/en-uk/node/863636); [MaPS research library entry](https://maps.org.uk/en/publications/research/2021/pensions-dashboard-user-research)
- MoneyHelper dashboard alpha assessment (GOV.UK): personas included "A problem for tomorrow" — "hesitant to engage with their pensions because of low expectations"; "research on trust led the team to test branding and design concepts for reassurance"; assessors told the team to keep testing around the user's question "So what does this mean and what should I do now…", to learn from HMRC's display of graphs, and to test coded options with assistive-technology users — [GOV.UK alpha assessment](https://www.gov.uk/service-standard-reports/money-helper-pensions-dashboard-alpha-assessment)
- Design standards: dashboards "must undertake user-testing" but may tailor "position on the page, branding, colouring, and font"; PDP acknowledged ERI "would need particular attention, as there is a lack of uniformity" across providers; PLSA: "some serious challenges remain to ensure that estimated retirement income values are consistent across DB and DC entitlements"; PLSA suggested that "showing a zero figure for ERI may be counterproductive" and a sub-£100 warning might be better — [Pensions Age on PDP consultation](https://www.pensionsage.com/pa/PDP-launches-pensions-dashboards-design-standards-consultation.php); [Corporate Adviser on PLSA](https://corporate-adviser.com/plsa-flags-problems-with-dashboard-design-standards/); [Pensions Age on ERI standards](https://www.pensionsage.com/pa/PDP-to-continue-defining-estimated-retirement-income-data-standards.php)
- PASA (Feb 2023): "very extensive testing with individuals with wide variances of pensions data" is needed, 12–24 months of it; standards "almost certainly need to change after testing"; ambiguity in displayed information will push queries onto scheme administrators — [Pensions Age, user testing could delay dashboards](https://www.pensionsage.com/pa/User-testing-could-delay-dashboards-available-point-until-2026.php)
- Later PDP saver research on consent wording: feedback "positive, particularly on clarity and conciseness, which encouraged respondents to read the on-screen text in detail" — [Pensions Age](https://www.pensionsage.com/pa/PDP-research-reveals-positive-feedback-as-industry-experts-emphasise-need-for-buy-in.php)

### Inferences
- The single question users bring ("what does this mean and what should I do now") maps exactly onto Someday's headline + ranked levers. The instrument's resting state should answer the first half; one gesture should reveal the second.
- The ERI consistency problem is Someday's advantage: a single engine computes one after-tax monthly figure from all six groups. Say so on the face of the instrument ("after tax, today's money") — the dashboards cannot.
- The "low expectations" persona argues for Path's tactic: never open with a shortfall. Open with the user's own wanted figure and show how long it holds.

### Gaps
- The Ipsos PDF and the PLSA PDF could not be text-extracted here; findings are from their HTML summaries and trade-press coverage. No published PDP comprehension data on ERI displays (percent correct, etc.) was found.

## Q6. Trust and credibility research for money tools: what makes a projection believable, and whether playful/3D presentation reduces trust

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced credibility drivers are surface quality and verifiability (Stanford: "design your site so it looks professional… appropriate for your purpose", "make it easy to verify"), and real-world studies of fintech apps find "good design begets credibility" while shoddy execution erodes trust in the institution itself. Playfulness per se is not shown to reduce trust; *reward* gamification (confetti, scratch cards) is what regulators and participants objected to. A glitchy animation is a trust event (Family's designer: it "erodes my trust").

### Cited Findings
- Stanford Web Credibility guidelines (3 years, 4,500+ participants): (1) "Make it easy to verify the accuracy of the information"; (6) "Design your site so it looks professional (or is appropriate for your purpose)" — "visitors judge a site quickly by its visual design"; (7) "Make your site easy to use — and useful… not by showcasing technology for its own sake"; (8) show content is reviewed recently; (10) "Avoid errors of all types, no matter how small"; the lab found participants "relied heavily on the surface qualities" — [Stanford Persuasive Technology Lab guidelines](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines); [Fogg 2002 summary](https://makinggood.ac.nz/methods/web-credibility/)
- Filene/UC Irvine pilot (27 participants aged 18–34, one month each with Mint, Wally, YNAB, Stash or Acorns): "good design begets credibility"; "poorly functioning or shoddy-looking apps eroded trust in the financial institution offering them"; "some participants felt that the gamification of the apps promoted addictive behaviors"; apps mostly raised awareness rather than changing behaviour — [IMTFI blog, Lessons of fintech apps design](https://blog.imtfi.uci.edu/2020/01/the-lessons-of-fintech-apps-design.html)
- Gamification literature: a 2025 Vietnam m-wallet survey (n=431) found gamification raised ease-of-use and attitude but "trust negatively influenced consumers' intentions to continue using"; Bayuk & Altobello (2019) found app users had higher *subjective* not objective knowledge; Rodrigues et al. (2015, n>800) found ease-of-use drove intention; none measured credibility as an outcome of playful visuals — [IJBM 2025](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/handle/11111/20317); [IJBM 2019](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJBM-04-2018-0086/full/html); [ISCTE e-banking game design](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/publications/the-introduction-of-game-design-in-e-banking-thinking-of-business-while-playing-in-a-virtual/84086)
- FCA consumer research: some people are disengaged because "they fear knowing the reality of their pension pots" (the "Ostrich effect"); Financial Lives highlights "lack of understanding and confusion about retirement income options" — [FCA bulletin via Financial Services Consumer Panel](https://fs-cp.org.uk/publication/data/data-bulletin-issue-12.pdf); [Professional Adviser on FCA report](https://professionaladviser.com/news/3019385/fca-report-significant-confusion-retirement-income)
- Behavioural Insights Team: a 2,822-person experiment with 22–29-year-olds found "prompting young people to think about their future made them care more about retirement"; a one-page Pension Wise "wake-up pack" raised take-up ~10% in trials, but a Standard Life trial saw under 1.5% of 15,753 customers click through; BIT's DWP work found members "engage more with how their funds perform financially than with other aspects" — [BIT Scottish Widows post](https://www.bi.team/?p=11897); [BIT Pension Wise trials PDF](https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Pension-wise-trials.pdf); [Corporate Adviser](https://corporate-adviser.com/nudge-unit-pension-comms-probe-backs-passport/); [DWP/BIT green pensions](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/applying-behavioural-insights-to-green-pensions/applying-behavioural-insights-to-green-pensions)
- Projection methodology debate: the FRC proposed growth rates "based on the volatility of each investment"; a commentator argued "a simple single rate would be easier for customers to understand" — [Actuarial Post](https://www.actuarialpost.co.uk/article/the-emotional-rollercoaster-behind-retirement-decisions-26232.htm)
- Family wallet's designer on motion and trust: money is "quite literally on the line, so every action… needs to be understood"; transitions between entering and confirming an amount "help users see that the value they entered is the value being sent"; "a glitchy animation in a banking app erodes my trust"; smooth motion gives "subconscious reassurance" — [Benji Taylor, "Family Values"](https://benji.org/family-values)
- Apple Card in Wallet: every open shows "total balance, a small chart of weekly activity and the date of their next payment"; controls "stay out of sight until needed"; the payment wheel "turns red" when a minimum payment would increase interest; an onboarding critic called the UI deliberately "cold" and found that empowering — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/bias-probe-or-not-the-apple-card-is-setting-a-new-standard-for-banks); [AppleInsider review](https://appleinsider.com/articles/19/09/01/review-apple-card-is-more-of-an-experience-than-a-reward-generator); [Apple Card onboarding UX review](https://medium.com/@pe8er/apple-card-onboarding-ux-a-review-cacffe55af95)

### Inferences
- The evidence does not say "3D reduces trust". It says *execution quality* and *purpose-fit* determine trust, and that *rewards for acting* read as manipulation. An instrument whose motion encodes the model (bands widening, sky dimming) is purpose-fit; an instrument that twinkles when touched is not. This is the same line the brief draws between "futuristic" and "gimmick".
- Stanford guideline 1 (verifiability) implies the instrument must expose its basis without leaving the screen: tax year, growth assumption, "2,000 futures", "today's money". Position these as fixed engraving on the instrument, not as a footnote.
- Stanford guideline 10 is the strongest argument for the brief's 60 fps requirement: a dropped frame on a money dial is an "error", and Family's designer reports exactly that reaction.
- Apple Card's red payment wheel is a precedent for *colour on a dial as a warning*, in the most conservative money UI there is.

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment manipulating 3D/skeuomorphic vs flat presentation of a *financial projection* and measuring trust was found. This is a genuine hole; recommend a quick A/B in the hallway test (brief §5D).

## Q7. Apple's money UIs, Family, Amie, Copilot: delight without clutter

### Takeaway
The documented recipe (Family) is: progressive reveal, motion that preserves spatial continuity ("we fly instead of teleport"), and delight concentrated in *rarely used* features while frequent actions stay quiet (the "Delight-Impact Curve"). Apple Card shows the quiet end: one balance, one small chart, a wheel that turns red. Copilot's praise is for calm and clarity, with no documented non-list interaction.

### Cited Findings
- Family's principles: Simplicity ("show fundamentals up front and reveal other features as they become relevant"), Fluidity ("We fly instead of teleport"; trays expand from the button that opened them and "vary in height so each step is clear"), Delight (the "Delight-Impact Curve": most delight in less-used features; "over-decorating frequently used features like sending tokens" is to be avoided); concrete details: "Continue" morphs into "Confirm" using shared letters; commas shift as you type an amount; scrubbing a price chart flips the arrow's direction; they avoided "static transitions that disrupt orientation", "redundant animations where an element duplicates itself", and "full-screen flows for brief actions" — [Benji Taylor, "Family Values"](https://benji.org/family-values)
- Family was acquired by Aave Labs (2023); Taylor later led design at Base and joined X in March 2026 — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/x-hires-crypto-veteran-benji-taylor-as-head-of-design-ahead-of-x-moneys-planned-april-launch/)
- Copilot Money: 2024 Apple Design Award *finalist* (not winner), Apple Editor's Choice; marketed as "polished and deliberately calm"; a 9to5Mac piece (sponsored) describes a dashboard of spending, recent transactions and monthly progress, and lists no gestures or non-list interactions — [Copilot App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1447330651); [Copilot FAQ](https://www.copilot.money/faq); [9to5Mac sponsored post](https://9to5mac.com/2025/06/26/copilot-money-is-the-budgeting-app-youve-been-looking-for/)
- Apple Card/Wallet: see Q6 citations; the Pratt Wallet critique praises security that is "integrated seamlessly" — [Pratt Wallet critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2021/09/design-critique-apple-wallet-ios-app/)

### Inferences
- Family's "trays vary in height so each step is clear" and "expand from the button that opened them" is the rule for Someday's five deeper views: each should open *from the segment of the instrument that owns it*, with a size that signals depth, never as a full-screen page.
- The morphing-label trick ("Continue"→"Confirm") suggests how Someday's headline can change state honestly: the £ figure stays, the qualifier morphs ("for life" → "to 84"), so the user sees exactly what changed.
- The Delight-Impact Curve says the *dial itself* (used constantly) should be quiet; a single moment of atmosphere (dawn when the plan completes) is permissible because completion is rare.

- Amie (calendar) — no source examined; not assessed. Apple Stocks/Apple Cash — no teardown found.

## Q8. Games whose rotational or object-based mechanics are learnable in seconds: Monument Valley, Fez, The Room, Gorogoa, Threes, Alto's Odyssey, Loop Hero, Tetris Effect, Device 6

### Takeaway
Every documented case reached "learnable without instructions" by *subtraction to one verb* and by making the object itself the only thing you can touch: Fez cut everything not about rotation ("design by subtraction"); Monument Valley cut free camera rotation so "you can see the whole world in one image"; The Room's whole design is "poke and prod the puzzle box"; Gorogoa forbade touching anything inside a tile; Threes spent a year "taking away ideas"; Alto rejected anything "requiring more than one touch input". Learnability comes from a fixed camera, one manipulable object, and immediate visible consequence.

### Cited Findings
- Monument Valley: an early version "let players zoom and rotate the camera around the structures, similar to Fez" — cut so the camera stays fixed in isometric view; the palette was three colours; Ken Wong: "We removed a lot of tools from the artist's toolbox"; "There's something really comforting about feeling like you can see the whole world in one image" — [Kill Screen, Monument Valley elegance](https://killscreen.com/monument-valley-elegance)
- ustwo replaced its original values (increasing difficulty, more content, more choice, clear instructions) with "Quality over quantity" and "Allow players to discover for themselves"; no budget or timeline; 1m+ sales at $3.99; Wong: 90 minutes of best work beats hundreds of hours of repetitive content — [Game Developer, quality before money](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/when-quality-comes-before-making-money-developing-i-monument-valley-i-)
- Each level was built around "its own core mechanism, like a specific type of rotation"; 30 levels cut to 10 — [Wallpaper*, Monument Valley at 10](https://www.wallpaper.com/tech/monument-valley-at-10-the-story-of-the-most-meticulous-puzzle-game-ever-created)
- Wong's GDC Europe 2014 talk "Less Game, More Experience": being embedded in a UX studio led the team to replace challenging puzzles with an aesthetic journey; every screen "a piece of art" — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020878/Designing-Monument-Valley-Less-Game); [Game Developer summary](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-designing-i-monument-valley-i-to-be-an-experience-not-a-game)
- Fez: rotation in 90° steps between four 2D views; Phil Fish adopted Ueda's "design by subtraction" — "There used to be a billion different things in Fez that didn't have to do with the core mechanic of rotation"; cut a weight system, health, enemies ("There was never a functional enemy in the game"); players are taught 2D platforming first, then rotation is unlocked as the fez; levels were made tall not wide because wide levels "move too much" when rotated — [Game Developer, Fez and Ico](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-polytron-s-i-fez-i-was-inspired-by-ueda-s-i-ico-i-); [Engadget 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011-09-28-fez-was-inspired-by-icos-minimalism-and-lonely-isolation.html)
- The Room: the second of three planned Unity prototypes, "Puzzle Box", was "a toy-like experiment about manipulating 3D objects with touch controls", loosely based on Chinese puzzle boxes and Zen Bound; one programmer and ten artists; under £80,000; 5m downloads, 2m+ premium sales; Barry Meade: "We worked hard on getting the control scheme right" and "we really wanted the game to be driven by the mechanics of the gameplay"; the camera "zoomed in on the box and reduced the surrounding chamber to a place to stand" — [PocketGamer.biz, making of The Room](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/thinking-outside-the-box-the-making-of-the-room/); [Pocket Gamer, Behind the touchscreen](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/053092/r/)
- Reviewers on The Room: "refreshingly tactile design… puzzle boxes that could very almost realistically exist"; "you rotate, slide, press, and twist components directly on the box" — [Digitally Downloaded review](https://www.digitallydownloaded.net/?p=43385); [games.gg](https://games.gg/the-room/)
- Gorogoa: Jason Roberts forbade direct manipulation of objects inside scenes — "there comes this temptation to create challenges that exist only within one tile"; "The source of all your power is where you choose to look"; "The handicapping of the player is part of what's interesting in design" — [Game Developer, rigid limitations](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-rigid-design-limitations-improved-i-gorogoa-i-s-puzzles); TouchArcade: "doesn't really have a tutorial, but it doesn't need one either… four panels in a grid that can be rearranged and zoomed" — [TouchArcade review](https://toucharcade.com/2017/12/13/gorogoa-review/)
- Threes: prototype Dec 2012; judged "not worth releasing" in Feb 2013; breakthrough "merge mode" June 2013; shipped after "a little over a year"; themes tried and cut (holes, flags, planets, atoms, monsters); Vollmer: most of the work was "taking away ideas"; a swipe moves tiles one space at most and spawns one tile; only six players had reached 6144 by March 2014; Apple Design Award 2014 — [Wired, Threes design](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/http://www.wired.com/2014/05/threes-game-design); [Game Developer on Wohlwend's minimalism](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-threes-i-co-creator-wohlwend-on-the-value-of-minimalist-game-design)
- Alto's Odyssey: "anything requiring more than one touch input did not feel right"; a grappling hook was cut for complicating one-touch play; wall-riding was adopted because it was "a one-touch addition to normal gameplay" — [iFanzine interview](https://www.ifanzine.com/?p=141975); [Pocket Gamer Q&A](https://www.pocketgamer.com/altos-odyssey/interview-a-closer-look-at-team-altos-stunning-sequel-altos-odyssey)
- Loop Hero postmortem: "tried to limit ourselves to a small number of elements, but polish each of them"; late-added bandit mechanics (final week) "made the mechanics harder to understand"; the original plan was "Four bosses, a camp, and a hero looping infinitely" — [Game Developer postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-loop-hero)
- Tetris Effect: two years' pre-production; Mizuguchi: the team changed the experience "without changing too many of the fundamental gameplay mechanics" — [Edge making-of](https://magzter.com/stories/Entertainment/Edge/The-Making-Of-Tetris-Effect); [GDC 2019 talk listing](https://enhance-experience.com/1328)
- Device 6: "everything in DEVICE 6's art, from the small tech blurbs, to the buttons, has an explanation tied to the narrative"; chapters are maps "built using text" navigated by swiping — [Simogo](https://simogo.com/work/device-6/); [USC Scalar](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/game-changers-2016-exhibit/device-6)

### Inferences
- The direct answer to "what makes a rotational mechanic learnable in seconds": (a) one object, fixed camera, the whole world visible at once (Monument Valley); (b) a single verb with an immediate, visible, reversible consequence (Fez, Threes); (c) the manipulable parts look like what they do — drums, dials, sliders on a box (The Room); (d) nothing else is touchable (Gorogoa). Someday's instrument must satisfy all four: the six input groups on one object, the camera never moving, turning any part changing the headline instantly, and no touchable chrome elsewhere.
- The brief's rejected cryptex ("too much hardware, everything said three times") failed (c) and the "one verb" rule — two rings with different semantics is two verbs. The Room works because *every* control on the box is a different physical verb but each is self-evidently what it is; the cryptex's rings looked identical and did different things.
- Fez's "tall not wide" finding is a concrete rendering rule: on a 390×844 portrait phone, a rotating object should be taller than wide, or edge content "moves too much" when turned.
- Monument Valley's cut of free camera rotation is a warning against letting the user orbit the instrument: rotation must be *of the object by the hand*, with the viewpoint fixed.

### Gaps
- The full GDC Vault talks (Monument Valley, Gorogoa "Cosmic Acrostic", Tetris Effect) are behind the Vault; only summaries were read. No designer statement on Monument Valley's teaching of rotation without text was found — the claim rests on third-party critical play and on the cut-content evidence.

## Q9. Radial wheels, pie menus and marking menus: evidence on speed and error

### Takeaway
The classic result (Callahan, Hopkins, Weiser & Shneiderman, CHI 1988) is that pie menus beat linear menus on seek time and error by fixing distance and enlarging targets (Fitts), with secondary sources reporting ~15% faster; Kurtenbach & Buxton found expert marks 3.5× faster than menu selection and eyes-free. The limits are equally firm: about eight items maximum, static contents only, poor labelling room, and weak discoverability. Six fixed inputs on a radial instrument sits comfortably inside those limits.

### Cited Findings
- Callahan et al. 1988 abstract: pie menus gain "by reducing target seek time, lowering error rates by fixing the distance factor and increasing the target size in Fitts's Law"; 15 menus × 4 trials per user; secondary reports put the speed gain at "as much as 15 percent faster… with fewer errors" — [UMIACS listing](https://sites.umiacs.umd.edu/node/16962); [pie menu history text](https://nic.funet.fi/pub/graphics/misc/news+mails/Comp.graphics/piemenues.txt); [OSNews retrospective](https://www.osnews.com/story/30371/pie-menus-a-30-year-retrospective/comment-page-2)
- Komerska & Ware (haptic 3D): "selection using the pie menu is considerably faster and more accurate than both types of linear menu" — [UNH scholars](https://scholars.unh.edu/ccom/295)
- Zhao & Balakrishnan (UIST 2004) cite marking menus as "up to 3.5 times faster" with a "seamless transition from novice to expert", and note that keeping errors under 10% forces fewer items per level in deeper hierarchies — [UIST 2004 PDF](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~ravin/papers/uist2004_simplemm.pdf) (PDF not text-extractable here; figures from search summary)
- Josh Clark: "Radial menus are faster to access than list-based menus"; every option equidistant (Fitts); "gestures get embedded in muscle memory, which is faster to access than visual memory"; eyes-free because selection depends on direction; "Eight seems to be the reasonable maximum"; large footprint "is costly on phones"; per Buxton, marking menus are inappropriate when item lists change; cites Buxton & Kurtenbach 1994: "Using a mark on average was 3.5 times faster than selection using the menu" — [Big Medium, radial menus for touch](https://bigmedium.com/ideas/radial-menus-for-touch-ui.html)
- Luís Abreu on gesture radial menus: "direction, not distance, identifies the item"; novices search visually, experts gain spatial knowledge only if the layout is stable; radial layouts "leave little room for text labels"; fingers obscure items; "a menu that is hard to find becomes a gimmick"; best example is Mass Effect mobile, which places wheels in the top corners away from the touch point, previews the selection at the touch point, and fades a trail on release — [lmjabreu.com](https://lmjabreu.com/post/gesture-based-radial-menus)
- Game weapon wheels: options equidistant from centre; "if you have more than eight options in your wheel, you probably either need another wheel"; players report mis-selecting neighbours in FPS wheels; pausing time "can alleviate some of the time pressure, but it also can break the player's immersion" — [300Mind, radial menus in game design](https://300mind.studio/blog/radial-menus-in-game-design/); [Giant Bomb thread](https://giantbomb.com/forums/radial-menu-210338/how-do-you-feel-about-weapon-wheels-1771617/); [UCF menu paper](https://www.eecs.ucf.edu/~jjl/pubs/Chertoff_final.pdf)
- Hades: players on Steam report the in-run boon menu is unexplained ("I don't know that the game explains how to use the menu of boons"); a community mod adds colour-coding "to avoid guesswork and reduce UI ambiguity" — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1145360/discussions/0/2245552720303899429); [Thunderstore mod](https://thunderstore.io/c/hades-ii/p/SMarBe/Improved_Boon_Info_UI/)

### Inferences
- Six input groups is inside the eight-item ceiling and the contents never change, which is exactly the regime where radial layouts earn their speed and muscle memory. A six-segment instrument is defensible on evidence; a twelve-segment one is not.
- The Mass Effect pattern — preview the selection where the finger is, keep labels away from the finger, confirm on release — is the touch-handling spec for a rotating dial on a 390 px screen.
- Labels are the weak point of radial layouts; the brief's "replace a label with position or motion" should be tested, since the literature says novices still search visually.

### Gaps
- No study of *continuous* radial input (turning a dial to set a value) versus linear sliders on touch was found in this strand; Fitts-for-dials belongs to the academic researcher's section.

## Q10. Weather apps as single-screen instruments: Carrot Weather, Weather Line, Dark Sky, Acme Weather, Mercury, Windy

### Takeaway
The weather genre proves a phone screen can hold now, next hours, next days and many variables without scrolling: Carrot 4.0 does it with three horizontal bands and in-place expansion; Dark Sky's vertical timeline was criticised precisely because it ran to "2.5 screens" and "felt crowded". The most relevant new precedent is Acme Weather (Dark Sky's founders, 2026), which draws *several plausible forecast lines* so that "tightly grouped lines suggest a dependable forecast; wide spread suggests the conditions could change" — a ready-made grammar for Someday's 2,000 futures.

### Cited Findings
- Carrot Weather 4.0: one screen — illustrated sky with actual/feels-like temperature, precipitation chance, wind and a one-line summary at top; hourly band in the middle; multi-day band at the bottom; swipe horizontally within a band; tap an hour/day to expand "in place, without leaving the main screen"; the hourly band is drawn as one continuous landscape; reviewer: it "doesn't feel overcrowded in any way… a noteworthy design achievement" — [MacStories review](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/carrot-weather-40-simply-delightful/)
- Dark Sky (2018 redesign): one vertical scroll — current conditions, radar thumbnail, precipitation bar for 24 h, hourly, 7-day — "about 2.5 screens on an iPhone X"; nine metrics shown as circles whose horizontal position shows change over time; the reviewer "isn't sold on the vertical timeline" and found "everything feels a little crowded", preferring Hello Weather's horizontal panels — [MacStories Dark Sky review](https://www.macstories.net/?p=55589); [Thurrott on the unified timeline](https://www.thurrott.com/mobile/166114/popular-weather-app-dark-sky-gets-major-revamp)
- Acme Weather (Feb 2026, $25/yr): the main forecast is "accompanied by several additional forecast lines, which show other outcomes the app considers plausible"; "the spread of these lines signals confidence"; Grossman: "Forecasts are often wrong", the app "embraces the idea that our forecast will sometimes be wrong", "We'll show you different possible futures, so you can be better informed" — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/23/new-weather-app-from-dark-sky-forecasts/); [Nieman Lab headline: an app "for uncertain, low-trust times"](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/02/the-team-behind-dark-sky-launches-a-weather-app-for-uncertain-low-trust-times/) (page returned 403; headline and search summary only)
- Mercury Weather (MacStories Best Design 2023): coloured backgrounds "convey information about the current conditions"; on iPad "the tiled interface shows everything at once"; user review: "One glance and you know… No digging and scrolling" — [MacStories Mercury review](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/mercury-weather-a-crystal-clear-design-for-every-apple-device/); [iMore](https://imore.com/apps/this-simple-iphone-weather-app-is-a-must-have-for-travellers)

### Inferences
- Carrot's "expand in place" is the no-scroll mechanism Someday needs for the five deeper views: tapping a band grows it on the same screen, rather than navigating.
- Acme's spaghetti-of-futures is the honest, legible way to put Monte Carlo on the face of the instrument: not a shaded band alone (which reads as certainty with margins) but a handful of thin paths whose spread *is* the confidence. Hullman/Kay's hypothetical outcome plots (academic strand) say the same thing from the research side.
- Dark Sky's failure is the brief's §2 failure: a vertical timeline becomes a scroll. Time must run *across* or *around*, not down.

### Gaps
- Weather Line and Windy: no design review located; Weather Line's horizontal temperature line is not evidenced here. Acme's actual screenshots were not viewable (403).

## Q11. Playful planners and trackers with one object: Apple Activity rings, Oura, WHOOP, Gentler Streak, Forest, Streaks, Nest

### Takeaway
Rings work because they are one object read at a glance and strictly protected (Apple's HIG forbids reusing them for other data), but their pull-to-close is a behavioural force: Gentler Streak refused rings because "rings tend to create a subconscious need to complete them". WHOOP's three-dial home earned praise for density and criticism that "new users may find the amount of information on the screen daunting". Forest makes risk physical through a single object that can die. The transferable principle: one object, one fill state, and risk shown as a visible change to that object — never as a score to chase.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Activity rings: designers "tried hundreds of interface designs"; the rings were chosen "the first time the team saw them… we knew that was it"; intended as "Simple, elegant, unique, easy to view, visually motivating and fun" (Jay Blahnik memo) — [Engadget 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015-03-10-apple-watch-activity-design.html); HIG: rings must sit on black, with clear margins, and must not be reused "as decoration or to show other data"; design the surrounding interface around the rings — [Apple HIG, Activity rings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings.md)
- Gentler Streak (2024 ADA Social Impact; 2022 Watch App of the Year): CEO Katarina Lotrič — "rings tend to create a subconscious need to complete them", conflicting with "adapting to where the user is, not the other way around"; cofounder Andrej Mihelič: "My problem wasn't that I lacked motivation. It was that I worked out too much. I needed something that let me know when it was enough"; "Statistics are just numbers. Without knowing how to interpret them, they are meaningless"; redesign cut four tabs to three — [BGR on rings](https://bgr.com/lifestyle/gentler-streak-gets-two-long-awaited-features-ahead-of-watchos-26); [Apple Developer news feature](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3m0ht22s); [BGR redesign](https://bgr.com/tech/gentler-streak-gets-a-major-redesign-focused-on-your-wellbeing/)
- WHOOP home (2023): "three dials for Sleep, Recovery, and Strain at the top of the Home screen, each with a deeper page"; review: "a sleek and useful improvement" but "new users… may find the amount of information on the screen daunting", "navigating to more detailed insights is not always intuitive", and the home links to 20+ screens — [WHOOP Locker](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/the-all-new-whoop-home-screen); [the5krunner review](https://the5krunner.com/2023/03/28/new-whoop-home-screen-looks-pretty-but-is-it-as-intuitive/)
- Oura: app surfaces four headline scores (Resilience, Readiness, Sleep, Activity) and lets users dig deeper; Oct 2025 redesign introduced a palette that "adjusts in real-time based on each member's biometrics" as an at-a-glance cue; some users called the redesign "very bad compared to the old UI" — [9to5Mac Oura diary](https://9to5mac.com/2024/07/22/oura-ring-diary-apple-ring/); [9to5Google on redesign](https://9to5google.com/2025/10/20/oura-app-redesign/); [FutureFive](https://futurefive.com.au/story/oura-app-redesign-brings-advanced-stress-cycle-insights)
- Forest: "a timer with stakes" — a tree grows while you focus and "leaving the app withers it"; user: "I'm not 'trying to focus,' I'm protecting my tree"; reviewers call it "a visual commitment" with "a minimalist design" — [Headway review](https://makeheadway.com/blog/forest-app-review/); [Lemon8 post](https://www.lemon8-app.com/experience/forest-focus-timer-app?region=us); [Sanitas app check](https://sanitas.com/en/magazine/developments-for-the-future/app-check/forest.html)
- Nest thermostat: "the front of the thermostat turns, so the device is, in effect, all dial"; Fadell moved complex functions (schedules) to the app, freeing the hardware from buttons; "Instead of changing it fifteen hundred times a year, do it 10 or 20 times and the Nest thermostat can learn from that"; Nest's later team "removed UI elements that weren't critical to the interaction of adjusting temperature" — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2013/02/15/114067/how-nests-control-freaks-reinvented-the-thermostat/); [Design Observer](https://designobserver.com/reinventing-the-thermostat/); [Nest design team on Medium](https://medium.com/building-for-the-home/designing-a-new-member-of-the-thermostat-family-36c81ebc5cb9) (403 on fetch; search summary only)
- Nest caution: a Nielsen Norman Group director's account of turning the dial to raise heat and getting "a confusing scheduled-temperature message instead of a clear status" — [Experientia blog](https://blog.experientia.com/?p=17671) (page unreachable at fetch time; search summary only)

### Inferences
- The ring debate is the sharpest guidance for Someday's confidence display: a ring that fills to 100% invites chasing certainty and reads as a score. Prefer Gentler Streak's logic — show *where you are relative to a band* — or Acme's spread of paths. If a ring is used, it must encode the share of futures that hold (a fact), not a target to close.
- WHOOP's three-dials-plus-twenty-screens is the failure mode of "one screen with depth": depth must be reached by one predictable gesture from the object, not by 20 links.
- Nest is the strongest commercial proof that a single dial can carry a household decision *and* the setting (schedules) can live elsewhere. Someday's equivalent: the dial carries the six inputs; year-by-year, tax and estate live in sheets.
- Forest shows risk made physical: the object itself changes state. For Someday, "how far it could fall" could be the instrument visibly dimming or the bands sagging under a named history — a change to the object, not a red badge.

### Gaps
- Streaks app: not examined. No Oura or Apple primary design rationale beyond the Blahnik memo and HIG. Nest-specific usability studies comparing dial vs buttons were not found (only general programmable-thermostat studies: LBNL found touchscreen tasks faster than buttons; Perry et al. tested five interfaces with 31 users) — [OSTI LBNL](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1048293); [LBNL affordances poster](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/bibcite/reference/32059).

## Q12. Synthesis: what from finance and games transfers to a one-screen, six-input instrument (for the report writer)

### Takeaway
Finance supplies the *honesty and trust rules*; games supply the *learnability rules*. Together they specify an instrument where the six inputs are fixed segments of one object (≤8, static — the radial-menu regime), the headline is the user's own monthly figure (Path's tone rule), uncertainty is drawn as a spread of futures on the face (Acme), depth opens in place from the segment that owns it (Carrot, Family), and the only "delight" is informational atmosphere at the rare moment of completion (Family's Delight-Impact Curve; the anti-confetti lesson).

### Cited Findings
- All findings above; key anchors: Fitts/pie-menu ceiling of eight static items ([Big Medium](https://bigmedium.com/ideas/radial-menus-for-touch-ui.html)); "see the whole world in one image" ([Kill Screen](https://killscreen.com/monument-valley-elegance)); "design by subtraction" ([Game Developer on Fez](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-polytron-s-i-fez-i-was-inspired-by-ueda-s-i-ico-i-)); Path's withheld shortfall ([Wealthfront](https://www.wealthfront.com/blog/introducing-path/)); Acme's spread-as-confidence ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/23/new-weather-app-from-dark-sky-forecasts/)); Stanford guidelines 1, 6, 7, 10 ([Stanford](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines)); "good design begets credibility" ([IMTFI](https://blog.imtfi.uci.edu/2020/01/the-lessons-of-fintech-apps-design.html)); Massachusetts exhibit list ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/robinhood-to-pay-75m-to-settle-massachusetts-charges/248214)); rings create "a subconscious need to complete them" ([BGR](https://bgr.com/lifestyle/gentler-streak-gets-two-long-awaited-features-ahead-of-watchos-26)); Toss's first-screen experiment ([Toss Feed](https://toss.im/tossfeed/article/importance-simplicity)).

### Inferences
- Scoring guidance against brief §6 for any candidate drawn from this strand:
  - *Different*: nothing in UK finance has inputs on a rotating object with live Monte Carlo; MoneyHelper, unbiased, PensionBee, Nutmeg are all forms (Q3). A radial six-segment instrument scores 5; a slider stack scores 1.
  - *One screen*: Carrot proves three bands + in-place expansion fits a phone (Q10); Dark Sky proves a vertical timeline does not. Any candidate with time running downward should lose points.
  - *One object*: The Room and Monument Valley show the camera must be fixed and the object the only touchable thing (Q8); the rejected cryptex lost because two rings looked alike and did different things.
  - *Live*: Path is the only finance precedent for input-and-projection in one gesture (Q2); Nest for dial-and-consequence (Q11).
  - *Honest*: Acme's multiple paths and Betterment's "96% chance" set the bar: a probability and a spread, visible at rest (Q2, Q10).
  - *Minimal*: Toss's measured null result for a busier first screen; Family's "nothing duplicated" (Q4, Q7).
  - *Legible in ten seconds*: radial labels are the known weakness (Q9); Hades' unexplained menu is the cautionary case. Budget hallway-test time for this specifically.
